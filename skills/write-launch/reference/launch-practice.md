# Launch practice

## Every kind

- Say what it is and what the reader can do with it first. Visitors often leave a page within 10 to 20 seconds, so the value has to land in the first 10.
- Write for scanning. In Nielsen Norman Group's test, 79 percent of users scanned a new page and 16 percent read it word by word.
- Objective beats promotional. Against a promotional page, an objective rewrite tested 27 percent more usable, and a concise, scannable, objective one 124 percent more. Readers distrust hype.
- Show one concrete example in place of a claim: a real command and its real output, or a number the repo backs.
- Start each heading with its most informative words. The first two carry the gist.
- Answer "why this over what I use now?" with one difference the reader can check.

## Show HN

- HN doesn't accept generated or AI-edited text in posts or comments. Never draft the title, the text, or a comment. Hand the user the facts to write from.
- Link a repo or a demo people can run. Never link a landing page, blog post, or sign-up page.
- Let people try it without a sign-up or an email.
- A version update or feature release usually doesn't qualify. A major overhaul may.
- The title starts with "Show HN". No uppercase or exclamation points for emphasis, no praise, no gratuitous number.
- The text sits at the top of the submission or in a first comment. It covers what it is, why it was built, what's different, and what's rough, in plain, factual words. Sales language turns readers off.
- Post from a personal account. Never ask anyone to upvote or comment.
- Repost only when it's substantially different, linking the earlier thread and saying what changed.

## Announcement post

- The key points go in the first two paragraphs.
- Use about half the words of conventional prose, one idea per paragraph.
- Bullets for lists, and link text that names its target.

## Landing page

- The first screen says what it is, what the reader can do with it, and what's different. The tagline states the offering, with no jargon and no "Welcome to".
- The most important content sits highest. Don't animate the headline.
- A page title runs 60 characters or less, keywords first.
